## How selection and weights were formed

The composite combines execution, industrial-policy support and industrial-network position using a
weighted multiplicative calculation. Companies below the full-universe lower-quartile threshold exit.
The surviving names receive weights in proportion to their network pillar within sector allocations:
62.5% to semiconductors and 37.5% to clean energy. This explains why a lower composite rank can still
have a larger portfolio weight. The signed evaluation gives the method, scores, thresholds and neutral
factor disclosures. The sample copies the recorded targets; it does not run new research or optimize them.

## Limitations

- **Neutral factors.** Subsidy utilization (P1a), capital-expenditure execution (P1c), the analyst-consensus
  narrative factor (P1d) and innovation spillovers (P3b) are each 0.5 for every company. They do not
  distinguish companies in this sample. Missing insider-ownership components are also neutralized where
  applicable; this does not invent an ownership percentage. The evaluation lists the affected companies.
- **Estimates and overrides.** GEV uses an estimate of return on invested capital from rounded June 30
  financial disclosures. TSM uses a native-currency calculation; GFS, MU and PLUG use analyst overrides
  where the standard feed could not supply the required value. These are disclosed methodological choices,
  not interchangeable with directly observed feed values.
- **Different observation dates.** October 3 analyst judgments are combined with the October 31 scorecard
  observations. The sample is not a historical October 3 backtest or a statement of what was then knowable.
- **Lineage and review.** The r4 inputs record that an earlier record of their approval lineage is
  unavailable and was not reconstructed, and that review, approval and authority checks were kept separate
  from them. This sample does not reconstruct that lineage or establish independent review, and it does not
  change the research's evidential status. The catalog lists this product's editions as published by their
  author, not independently reviewed.
- **Evidence.** The inputs identify each source by an identifier, a publication date and a content hash. The
  original third-party documents are not redistributed, and the edition lists no evidence links.
- **Reproducibility.** The targets are a compiled output of this research; a fresh run with other dates or
  data providers could select a different portfolio.
- **Execution.** Whole-share execution leaves cash, so a portfolio can differ from these ideal weights,
  especially for a small allocation. The account used for offline previews is fictional, with illustrative
  prices of exactly USD 100 for each included company; these are not market prices, forecasts or valuations.

## Method

US industrial-policy three-pillar scoring and sector-weighted portfolio, version 0.3.2.

This archived Q4 2026 sample uses three normalized pillars. P1 covers execution: subsidy utilization, return on invested capital relative to the cost of capital, capital-expenditure execution, the analyst-consensus narrative factor, governance and political-economy exposure. Its component weights are 25%, 25%, 20%, 12%, 10% and 8%, respectively. P2 combines the type and scale or materiality of eligible industrial-policy support with an assessed policy-risk reduction. P3 combines industrial-network position (60%) and innovation spillovers (40%). Neutral factors and company-specific estimates are disclosed separately.

The composite is a weighted multiplicative blend of P1 + 0.01, P2 + 0.01 and P3 + 0.01, bounded to \[0, 1\] and rounded to four decimal places. Pillar weights vary with quarters since inception; this scorecard uses one quarter, giving approximately 32.1727% to P1, 49.9913% to P2 and 17.8360% to P3. Thus the composite is not an arithmetic weighted sum. The component and pillar scores are also rounded to four decimal places in the recorded calculation.

Selection thresholds use the full ten-company universe. Scores below its 25th-percentile threshold exit. Re-entry requires a score strictly above the median for two consecutive quarters; this edition has no re-entries. The recorded 75th-percentile flags do not add an overweight bonus in this edition.

Portfolio weights use P3 among surviving companies within each active sector, rather than the composite rank. The active semiconductor and clean-energy budgets are normalized to 62.5% and 37.5%, respectively. Within each sector, P3-proportional weights are constrained to 0.5 to 1.5 times equal weight; a 35% portfolio-wide single-name cap also applies. Neither bound changes this edition's seven resulting targets. Inactive-sector budgets are redistributed proportionately across the active sectors. The exact published targets are copied from the original r4 output without recalculation or new optimization.

## Defaults disclosed

- Subsidy-utilization component P1a is fixed at the neutral score 0.5 for all ten companies. This is a factor treatment, not an assertion that half of an award has been drawn or paid.
- Innovation-spillover component P3b is fixed at the neutral score 0.5 for all ten companies. The effective patent, standards and licensing inputs used to obtain that value are neutral scoring assumptions, not observed innovation measurements.
- The insider-ownership component within P1e is set to 0.5 for GEV, GFS, INTC and TXN because an approved exact ownership fraction was unavailable. No synthetic ownership percentage is substituted; the other companies retain their recorded inputs.
- Capital-expenditure execution component P1c is 0.5 for every company because no usable capital-expenditure plan was supplied to this scoring step. This does not mean actual capital expenditure or execution was zero.
- Analyst-consensus narrative component P1d is 0.5 for every company because an approved consensus-target source was unavailable. The neutral factor is not an analyst forecast.
- GEV return on invested capital uses the recorded rounded-disclosure estimate for the June 30, 2026 measurement date, rather than a direct feed observation. TSM uses the recorded native-currency calculation. GFS, MU and PLUG use recorded analyst ROIC overrides to address feed or coverage gaps. These choices are retained from r4.
